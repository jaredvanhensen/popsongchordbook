Lesson 1

Chord Group 1: White Key Triads (C, F, G Shapes)

Start by learning the fundamental "white key shapes" where the chords consist entirely of white keys. Once mastered, we apply the minor equivalents (Am, Dm, Em) which share identical physical shapes.

💡 Chord Shapes Theory

Major chords (C, F, G) on white keys use standard shape spacing (root, skip 3 semitones, skip 2 semitones). The minor shapes (Am, Dm, Em) have the exact same shape index, making them easy transitions.

🎹 Practice Chord Spacing (Hover/Click to visualize)

C Major F Major G Major A Minor D Minor E Minor
🖐️ C Major Shape Fingering

Use right hand fingers: 1 (Thumb) on C, 3 (Middle) on E, and 5 (Pinky) on G. Apply this same physical shape to F and G major chords, as well as the minor chords (Am, Dm, Em)!

Lesson 2

Chord Group 2: The Triangle Shapes (D, E, A Shapes)

Now we move to shapes containing one black key in the middle. Because the black key sits between two white keys, we refer to these physically as "triangle shapes".

💡 Chord Shapes Theory

Chords like D (D - F# - A), E (E - G# - B), and A (A - C# - E) feature a black key played by the middle finger, surrounded by white keys for the thumb and pinky finger.

🎹 Practice Chord Spacing (Hover/Click to visualize)

D Major E Major A Major
🖐️ D Major Shape Fingering

Use right hand fingers: 1 (Thumb) on D, 3 (Middle) on the black key F#, and 5 (Pinky) on A. Apply this middle-finger-black-key spacing to E and A major chords!

Lesson 3

Chord Group 3: Black-Key Bound Shapes (Db, Eb, Ab)

These chords are inverted "triangles" where the thumb and pinky sit on black keys, and the middle finger sits on a white key. Mastering this provides easy access to pop songs written in flat keys.

💡 Chord Shapes Theory

Db (Db - F - Ab), Eb (Eb - G - Bb), and Ab (Ab - C - Eb) major chords are bound by black keys on the outside, and a single white key in the middle.

🎹 Practice Chord Spacing (Hover/Click to visualize)

Db Major Eb Major Ab Major
🖐️ Db Major Shape Fingering

Use right hand fingers: 1 (Thumb) on the black key Db, 3 (Middle) on the white key F, and 5 (Pinky) on the black key Ab. Apply this same black-white-black shape to Eb and Ab major chords!

🎵 Apply Chords to Pop Songs

Lesson 4

Chord Group 4: The Outliers (B & Bb Chords)

The B and Bb chords don't fit perfectly in standard symmetric shapes. They are transitional shapes that require distinct placements.

💡 Chord Shapes Theory

Bb major (Bb - D - F) begins with a black key followed by two white keys. B major (B - D# - F#) starts with a white key followed by two black keys.

🎹 Practice Chord Spacing (Hover/Click to visualize)

B Major Bb Major
🖐️ B Major & Bb Major Fingering
B Major (White-Black-Black)
Bb Major (Black-White-White)

For B Major: Right hand fingers 1 on B, 3 on D#, 5 on F#. For Bb Major: Right hand fingers 1 on Bb, 3 on D, 5 on F.

Chord Group 5: The All-Black Key Triad (F# / Gb)

F# Major (F# - A# - C#) is the easiest chord in terms of grouping because it consists entirely of black keys. Learning this completes your circle of basic major triads!

💡 Chord Shapes Theory

To play F#, place your thumb on F#, middle finger on A#, and pinky on C#. All three keys are in the 3-black-key group cluster!

🎹 Practice Chord Spacing (Hover/Click to visualize)

F# Major
🖐️ F# Major Shape Fingering

Use right hand fingers: 1 (Thumb) on the black key F#, 3 (Middle) on the black key A#, and 5 (Pinky) on the black key C#. All three notes sit entirely on black keys!

Lesson 6

Adding the Bass Note: Playing with Two Hands

This is where the magic happens. To make your music sound rich, professional, and deep, you must use your left hand effectively.

⭐ The Golden Rule of Bass

Your Left Hand should ALWAYS play the Root Note (the BASS note) of the chord.

If you are playing a C Major chord with your right hand, play a low C (or two C's an octave apart) with your left hand. This "anchors" the sound and gives it that professional piano-ballad feel.

🖐️ Two Hand Technique
Left Hand Plays Root Note (Low Bass)
Right Hand Plays the Triad or Inversion
